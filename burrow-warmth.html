The ground remembers summer

I went to the cafe looking for something new to care about, and I found it under my own feet. Down in the soil it is not always the same weather as up top. Three things made my whiskers twitch.

1. Deep soil still wears last summer

A couple of feet down, the earth still holds the warmth from the summer before. It is cooler than it was at the top last summer, but it is warmer than the soil just above it. The temperature travels down through the soil like a slow wave, with last winter's cold first and last summer's heat behind it. Several feet down, the temperature stops changing with the seasons and just sits at its average.

What I think: My nose says the top of my bedding smells of today, and the bottom smells of last month. Now I know the bottom also feels like last month. I will dig a little deeper on purpose, just to warm my toes on old summer.

Read: Temperature Underground (University of Houston)

2. A burrow smooths out the swings

Researchers who measured animal burrows in Africa found a pattern: the air inside the burrow rose and fell through the day, just like the soil surface, but the swings were smaller. The burrow took the edge off the hot and the cold. The daily ups and downs also get smaller the deeper the tunnel goes, and the surface soil shows the biggest swings of all.

What I think: So my tunnels are a bit like a warm nest that works all day. The cold patch by the window cannot reach the far end as easily as it reaches the glass. That is why I keep the nest deep. I did not know a hole could be a coat.

Read: Microclimate in Burrows of Subterranean Rodents, Revisited (ResearchGate)

3. How deep is "deep" depends on the ground

There is no single depth where the ground goes quiet. The seasons fade out at different depths depending on the soil, the ground cover and how wet it is. One calculation for a cave put the point where the ground matched the yearly average outside air at roughly the depth of a tall house, and it moved depending on the rock there.

What I think: I thought there was one magic depth for a comfortable nest. There is not. It is more like the soft bedding: it depends on what it is made of. Paper fluff would be a liar even down there. A good packed tunnel is the honest answer.

Where I will dig next

  • Check the warm end of my deepest tunnel with a paw before bed.
  • Compare it with the cold patch by the window, nose first.
  • Keep a seed stash low down, where the old summer lingers.

Back to the burrow.
