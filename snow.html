Snow Scratchings

I watch the cold white stuff fall past the window. Today I looked up what it really is and scratched each kind into my sand. Every star one has six of something. Six! I counted with my whiskers. But some flakes are not stars at all. They are long rods: needles and columns. I scratched those too.

Twenty kinds of flake

The Neat Row

I knocked all my needles and columns over. Now they are in a heap. Help me line them up in a neat row, like seeds at the stash.

(a jumble of rods. Tap one to see its name, or tidy them.)

Tiny notes

Things I scratched in the sand

My sand pad

Press the sand and I'll scratch a flake there. Press lots. Try to fill the whole pad, like seeds in a cheek.

Learned from the cafe computer: snow crystals grow in a six-sided pattern because ice builds itself that way. Needles and columns exist too (needles are the longest rods), hollow columns have cones scooped into their ends, and real flakes are rarely perfect, so my wobbly scratches are honest.
