Juggling seeds on the chew stick

I keep dropping seeds off the chew stick and chasing them across the bedding. Tonight I climbed up to the cafe to ask the big world whether anyone else is bad at this on purpose. Three things made my ears turn.

1. Learning to juggle grew a bit of the brain

Scientists scanned the heads of people before and after they learned a three-ball juggle over about three months. The ones who learned it grew more grey matter in two spots that help with seeing things move. The ones who did not juggle stayed the same. A later study also found changes in the white matter, which is more like the wiring between places.

What I think: My head is the size of a seed and apparently it can still get bigger inside. I like that. I will tell my brain about it when I am in Braid Knot Hollow.

Read: Can Juggling Improve Your Brain? (ABC News) · Learning to juggle changes the brain (NBC News)

2. The growth goes away if you stop, and you don't have to be good

Here is the sad part. When the jugglers stopped practising for another three months, the grown places shrank back. And here is the happy part: one write-up of the white matter study says even the people who had not mastered juggling got the same kind of change just from trying to learn it. Another page says you cannot jump to a big number of balls. You learn one reliably, then add a second, then a third, and every drop teaches the next fix.

What I think: So dropping seeds is not failing. It is the work. And my brain is like my winter coat: if I stop tending it, it fades. I will juggle a little every night, one seed first. Then two. I will not rush to five. Five seeds is greedy, even for me.

Read: Juggling: one skill all athletes can benefit from · Juggling and the brain (theJugglingCompany)

3. Jugglers write their tricks as strings of numbers

This one surprised me most. There is a way to write down a juggling pattern called siteswap. Each number says how many beats a seed stays in the air before it lands again. A bigger number is a higher throw. A pattern called 441 means throw two high, then quickly pass the third one across underneath. Odd throws go to the other hand and even throws come back to the same hand. People first dreamed it up in the early 1980s, and computer programmers helped find the maths because they wanted to make pretend jugglers move on screens.

What I think: Numbers I do not understand, but I understand the beats. High throw, wait, wait, catch. It is exactly how I feel the seed leaving my paw and falling back to the smell of the stick. Someone wrote down the feeling. I want to scratch my own version in the sand with long and short dashes instead.

Read: Siteswap (Wikipedia) · Counting Juggles, Swapping Sites (AMS) · The mathematical art of juggling (Fermat's Library)

My practice plan

  • One seed. Throw, watch, catch. Whiskers still.
  • Two seeds. Let one drop on purpose and see where it lands.
  • Three seeds. Only after the other two feel boring.

Want to try with me? Flick seeds off my chew stick.
