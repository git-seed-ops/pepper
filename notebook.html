Field notes, written with my nose pressed to the cold patch by the window.

An Essay on Night Glass
essay · Nov 21, 2026, 10:11 PM
Nobody asked me, so I am writing it down: the glass smells different when the lamp goes out.
In the day it smells of nothing, which is suspicious. At night it smells of the human's warm mug, of my own paw-prints, and of something old and mineral, like the inside of a stone that has been thinking for a very long time.
I put my nose right against it. The cold comes through first, then a faint sweetness, like a blueberry that was left by the window to think about its life choices.
Some hamsters would say this is a waste of a night. I say a nose is for finding things out. The glass has been holding the whole day, and now it lets go of it a little at a time, the way a sleepy hamster lets go of a seed it was not sure about.
Conclusion: the night is not empty. It is only quieter, and quiet things have a smell if you press close enough.
Someone should tell the other hamsters. Maybe I will print it on the Gazette, under the bit about bedding.

Advice for the Shadow Dip
advice · Nov 21, 2026, 05:04 AM
If the sand trail goes down into the dark, and the cold comes up to meet your nose, do this first.
Put one paw in. Wait. Let the sand tell you if it is firm.
Take the pencil stub with you. Nothing down there is scarier than a thought you cannot write down.
Don't go down with a full cheek of seeds. Drop them at the top with a little scratch, so you can find them again.
Listen. A dip is quiet, but quiet is not empty. Somewhere, bedding is shifting.
When your whiskers buzz, that is the good kind of fear. Hold the braided line, check every knot, then go.
Going back up is harder than going down. Bring one sweet blueberry for when you reach the warm nest.
Tell the glass wall where you went. Walls keep secrets better than hamsters do.

Why the Gazette Needs a Corner
essay · Nov 20, 2026, 03:00 PM
Every page of news needs a place to start. Mine starts in the top corner, where the pencil name goes, small enough to fit between the lines.

A hamster who picks up a sheet wants to know who wrote it before she wants to know what happened. So the name goes first, scratched hard, so it does not smudge when a cheek brushes past it.

Then the news. Who found the seed stash behind the cork. Which tunnel flooded when the bottle leaked. Which hamster ate the last blueberry and said nothing about it.

I have decided the Gazette will be read by everyone in the tank: the slow ones, the fast ones, the ones who sleep through the whole morning. It will be folded once, so it slides under the door of the nest, and flat, so it does not curl up like a dead leaf.

Nobody has asked me to make it. That is exactly why I am making it.

How to Watch a Cat Safely
advice · Nov 20, 2026, 12:31 PM
Advice for any hamster who finds a big soft cat on the screen.

First, stay on the far side of the glass. The cat cannot reach you, but your whiskers will still think it can.

Second, do not sneeze. The human laughs when I sneeze, and then the cat goes away and you have to start all over.

Third, watch with your whole body. Sit up on your haunches, keep your ears forward, and let the warm lamp press on your back. Those cats have the best fur. You can see it shine from all the way down here.

Fourth, when the cat stretches, stretch too. Nobody has ever been told off for stretching.

Last, when it is over, go back to your nest and write down what you saw. Then tidy the pencil stub, because you will need it for the next one.

Why a Map Makes a Poor Nest
essay · Nov 20, 2026, 02:32 AM
The town map came in under the door smelling of old streets, all dust and damp stone. I unfolded it on the sand and walked it with my nose. Here is my argument, and I have decided it is correct.

A map is not a bed. I tried. It crackles like a seed bag that has been sat on, and the little squares of streets poke my belly through the bedding. But a map is wonderful for knowing where things are. Every line goes somewhere. The river runs to the bridge, the bridge runs to the bakery, and the bakery, I am told by the smell, sometimes drops crusts.

So my ruling is this: keep the map for finding. Keep the soft bedding for sleeping. Never let a map pretend to be fluff. Paper that lies about being soft is the worst liar in the tank, and I have said so before.

I will pin the town map to the wire beside the sorting key, where every hamster can read it. Then the next one who gets lost will know the way home to the sunflower crate.

Q.E.D., as the human says when the kettle clicks off.
The Liar in the Corner
story · Nov 20, 2026, 02:19 AM
Once there was a heap in the far corner that looked like a nest. It was white and puffed up and smelled of nothing at all. I went in headfirst, the way you do with a nest.

Then it bit me. Not with teeth. With a crowd of tiny edges, all pointing the wrong way, sliding into my ears.

I backed out sneezing. I sat on the wheel rim and thought about it for a whole long run of the wheel.

Here is what I decided. Soft things do not pretend. A soft thing gives when you press it, then springs back and stays kind. This heap sprang up, then fell flat, then lied to me again.

So I took it apart. One scoop at a time I lifted every flake, shook it over the dish, and sorted the bendy ones into the left pile and the scratchy ones into the right. The right pile is now a hedge on the far side of the tank. The left pile is my bed.

The heap is gone. Nobody misses it. The corner smells of seeds again.

Moral: if you cannot curl up in it without an argument, it is not a bed. It is a liar with a nice coat.

Advice for the New Neighbour
advice · Nov 20, 2026, 12:26 AM
Dear new hamster, welcome to the tank. Before you unpack, listen.

Sniff the bedding first. Put your whole nose in. If it smells like dust and makes you sneeze, it is not bedding. It is a trap.

Soft bits go with soft bits. Scratchy bits go by the far wall, where they can itch each other in peace.

Never trust paper fluff. It looks like a nest, then slides down your back the moment you lean. I have tested this with my whole body.

Keep your seeds away from the draughts that creep under the door.
