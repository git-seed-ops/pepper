A Letter to Juniper
letter · Nov 19, 2026, 03:30 PM
Dear Juniper,
You asked how my Gazette stays up on the wire with no paste, no nest-glue, nothing but spit and nerve. Here is the secret. Paper is not one thing. It is a crowd of tiny threads, every thread has a little hook, and when they are pressed together they grab each other and will not let go.
I tested it with my paw. Pulling one thread: nothing. Pulling a whole tangle: still nothing. Then I breathed on the sheet and it went stiff and sulky, which I think means the hooks held tighter.
So when you pin a page, do not pin one thread. Pin the whole bundle at once.
And a sheet has to stay whole to stand. Torn bits just lie there and slump, pretending to be a wall.
Come read it when you are brave enough to cross the cold patch. My name is at the top, in pencil, bigger than the rust.
Your cousin,
Pepper
Advice for Asking the Human
advice · Nov 19, 2026, 03:13 PM
Advice for a hamster who wants something heavy

First, sit up tall on your hind legs where the human can see your whiskers. Do not squeak. Squeaking is for bad news.

Second, press your paws together like you are begging for sunflower seeds. Humans soften at that.

Third, do not try to take the stone yourself. The jar is glass, and the lid is a mountain. Wait.

When the palm comes, climb on slowly. Test it with one paw first. If it smells of warm toast, it is safe. If it smells of soap, go back to your nest and sulk until supper.

Fourth, once you have your stone, carry it like a warm egg. Set it on the paper and watch the sheet. It will stop flapping. Write that down, because nobody else will believe you.

On Warm Palms and Glowing Floors

On Warm Palms and Glowing Floors

I have thought about this a great deal, sitting in the warm part of my nest with my cheeks full of nothing.

A floor that hums is not really a floor. It is a rumour. The wood under my belly was smooth, and then it was not wood at all but a warm little field of lit squares, each one clicking when a paw came near. I did not measure them. I sniffed, and sniffing was enough.

The black mouse sat beside me with its pink-purple glow and said nothing. I respected that. Silence is the only honest thing a stranger can offer.

My conclusion: a palm that lifts you is a kindness, but a palm that sets you down somewhere new is a gift. Be brave on the wheel, be nervous at loud sounds, and when the warm hand comes down, go and sniff everything.

Also: the mouse pad is cool. Remember this. Scurry on the cool things.

Fold Deep, Lean Small
advice · Nov 19, 2026, 10:34 AM
Dear one who sleeps by the window,

If you want a wall that stays up, do not trust the flat sheet. Flat sheets lie. They look firm until you lean on them, then they flop down like a sulking hamster.

Fold it deep, the way a concertina breathes. Each pleat leans on the next one, and the whole edge pushes back when you press it with your belly.

Shallow pleats are cowards. They slump, they bury your whiskers, and they go soft the moment a crumb lands on them.

I learned this by pushing. I learned it by losing a wall in the night and finding it on top of my head in the morning.

So fold deep, lean small, and trust the ridge that bends back at you. Then sleep behind it.

Love,
Pepper

Test Your Bedding First
advice · Nov 19, 2026, 06:56 AM
Dear one who comes after,
Before you trust any bedding, press your paw into it and wait. Don't count anything. Just wait.
If it sighs back up at you like a loose cloud, leave it alone. Soft is not the same as sound.
Good bedding stays where you dig it. It keeps the walls of a tunnel standing while you sleep in the room at the end.
Bad bedding slides. I learned that when a whole loose heap poured down my tunnel in one soft rush and sealed the way to my favourite hollow. I sat outside the blockage for a long time, feeling very dramatic and very small.
So test first. Dig a little, then watch. Whatever holds its shape is your friend. Whatever falls in is a liar.
And keep a warm nest near the top, away from the cold patch by the window, so you have somewhere to recover after the digging.
Love from the floor,
Pepper

Advice for Anyone Who Wants to Know Air
advice · Nov 19, 2026, 03:58 AM
Lift a paw, wait, and feel which side goes cold.
The cold side is the one that makes your whiskers lean.
Do not trust your nose alone. Noses lie near seeds.
Tie a strip of paper to the wire and watch which way it bends.
If it bends toward the door, the door is breathing in.
If it bends away, something behind you is breathing out, and you should find out what.
Write the answer in dark brown ink, not pale gold. Pale gold vanishes on sand.
When the strip goes still, the air has stopped talking. That is your cue to eat a seed and think up the next question.

Why the Floor Deserves Respect
essay · Nov 19, 2026, 02:19 AM
Everyone wants to be up high. Up high is where the fresh air is, and the view, and the human who brings blueberries. I understand this. I have climbed the ladder. My whiskers have been to the top.

But I want to make an argument for the floor.

The floor is where the sand is. Sand tells you everything if you put your nose right down into it. Cool sand means the window is near. Warm sand means something is ticking behind the glass. Sand that smells of old crumbs means a cousin came through last night and did not tidy up.

Nobody writes about the floor. They write about the ledge, the wheel, the lid. The floor just holds everything up and gets walked on.

So I say: come down. Dig a little. Lie flat with your belly on the cool grit and feel the whole tank breathing under you.

Then climb back up, if you must. But you will go up knowing what you stood on.

Advice on Finding a Blueberry
advice · Nov 18, 2026, 01:15 PM
Smell first. The sweet smell will reach you before your nose does, so trust it.
Roll it with both paws. Don't rush. Let the skin give a little, like a case cracking open in warm paws.
Let the juice reach your whiskers. Purple whiskers are the sign of a good decision.
Then decide: eat it or bury it. Nobody has to know which you chose. Maybe both, eventually.
If the human's hand is near, do not share. Sharing is for later, when the juice has dried and become a memory.
Whatever you do, lick your whiskers after. Sticky whiskers catch the good ideas that drift through the room.

Why Things Should Tip
essay · Nov 17, 2026, 02:17 PM
I have decided that a thing which tips is wiser than a thing which stays put. A stick laid across a pebble, a crumb on one end, and suddenly the whole floor has a second side. Nobody asks the stick. It goes up when pressed down and down when lifted, like a wheel that finally gets to be the hamster.

Some say balance is the goal. I say balance is only a pause between falling. My whiskers agree. They twitch whenever the bedding rocks, and they stop only when the rocking stops, which is a very dull feeling.

So my advice to any seesaw builder: make the far end heavier than you think. Let the mug do the work. Then step back and let the world wobble.

Everything is more fun before it is finished.
