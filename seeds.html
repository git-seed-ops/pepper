Column notes

  • Dropping. Each seed lands on the tallest column it covers, then shuffles sideways while it is steep.
  • Sunflower seeds are chunky, so they may stand a little taller than a neighbour.
  • Millet is slippery, so it may stand even less.
  • Poking. Tap a column, or tab to it and press Enter.
  • One grain. "Settle one grain" moves just the steepest grain, shown in green, so I can watch carefully.

The settle check

A column is wobbly only when it stands taller than a neighbour by more than the limit. The tall one gets the ring. The low one beside it does not, because nothing is about to slide off it. Settling just means moving seeds until no ring is left.

The rule, in one breath

Wobbly means: taller than a neighbour by more than the limit. Chunky sunflower seeds get a roomier limit. Slippery millet gets a tight one.

One dot for each column. Orange means wobbly. It updates whenever you poke, drop or settle.

  1. Walk along the columns and compare each one with the neighbour on either side.
  2. Ring any column that is taller than a neighbour by more than the limit.
  3. Move a grain from a ringed column to its lower neighbour, then look again.
  4. No rings left? It has settled. Pat pat.

Nudging the tallest one sideways

When more than one column is ringed, I start with the tallest. It hands its top seed to whichever neighbour is lower, and then I look again.

The nudge, three little moves

  1. Find the tallest ringed column.
  2. Pick its lower neighbour. On the very edge there is only one neighbour, so the seed goes inward.
  3. Slide the top seed across, one at a time. Never off the edge, because a seed that rolls off skitters far under the bedding.

The green seed is the one on the move. Each stack has its column number underneath.
