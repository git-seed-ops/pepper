Silk Trail Nook: where a web gets fixed

Back to the burrow

What I found in the nook

This time I looked at orb weavers, the big round-web spiders, and what they do after a strand breaks. Some of it was not what I had guessed. These are the bits that stuck.

1. The sticky parts are the ones left alone

An old lab study put orb spiders in boxes and broke their webs. The repair threads were all the plain, non-sticky kind, the dragline. The spider never swapped out a damaged spoke, the sticky spiral that catches bugs, or the middle where the lines meet. It fixed only what it had to, with as little silk as it could. I thought a spider would want to rebuild the catching part. It does not.

I tried this with my own sand. I smoothed a patch, then dragged one bit of grit through the middle of it. I left the sticky-feeling crumbs where they were and only patched the hole.

2. The spider reacts to the tension, not the tear

A later write-up says the spider responds to a radial thread going slack. The web can be cut in a place that does not feel tight, and the spider may not come running. What it senses is the pull on its frame. I found that odd, since I always go for the place that smells of a break. Spiders seem to read the web like my wheel: you feel the shake through your feet, not the crack you cannot see.

3. Wind makes them quick, and sloppy

A study with wind blowing across the webs found that the spiders started repairing sooner, as if they had expected trouble. The repair still took about as long, but it did not work as well. The web came back, just not as good. I think that is a fair trade if the wind keeps coming, and a bad one if it does not.

What I think of it

I like that a spider keeps the parts that work and only patches the rest. My bedding is the same. A good corner stays put when the rest gets kicked around. I used to think a fix had to be a whole new thing. Now I think a small patch in the right place might be the better job.

The wind part worries me a little. Being quick is not the same as being right. I have been rushing to the seed pile every time the draught moves, and maybe I should wait and check first, like the spider does with its line. I will try that next time the cold patch by the window starts to creep.
